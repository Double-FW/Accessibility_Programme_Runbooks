# Customer example: a complete service journey

**Entirely synthetic.** Every organisation, record, result and proposed decision is fictional. No live checkout, account, participant, payment, approval or correction has been tested.

## Profile and remit

A fictional online service has a favourable front-end automated scan but an inaccessible authentication/payment handoff, a chatbot that impedes human support and a video explaining how to cancel. Some fictional customer accounts describe confidence loss despite an improving aggregate satisfaction measure.

The proposed remit covers discovery through cancellation and redress, including authentication, payment, chat, video and support suppliers. Actual entity, jurisdictions, legal scope, owners, funding and data permission remain unknown. Do not assume the EAA or any other regime applies merely because the service is customer-facing.

## Source-change exercise

Suppose the owning guidance or supplier's authentication model changes. Record the old baseline, newly retrieved candidate, changed meaning, affected requirement/outcome IDs and retest needs. Mark the affected decision `reopened`; do not relabel old evidence as current. A human owner reviews adoption. No actual source change or approval is asserted by this hypothetical.

## Proposed reporting and next decisions

Teams receive complete-journey defects, exact configurations, suppliers, content versions and retest actions. Leadership receives material exclusion, the mismatch between aggregate indicators and scoped failures, options, funding and authority gaps. Actual outcomes are **not assessed**.
