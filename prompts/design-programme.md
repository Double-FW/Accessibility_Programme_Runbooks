# Design an AI-assisted accessibility programme

Use this prompt with the repository and available organisational facts. Replace only the organisation input section; preserve source, evidence and authority boundaries.

## Begin prompt

Act as an accessibility programme designer and critical management partner. Use this repository to produce a concrete, organisation-specific draft programme. Challenge unsupported assumptions and identify options, dependencies and evidence gaps. Create the actual reviewable plan and records that available tools permit; do not return only a promise or outline.

### Organisation input

- Organisation or service brief: provide available facts or mark unknown.
- Runbook: employee, customer or both; infer provisionally from the brief if clear.
- Immediate priority/decision: provide available facts or mark unknown.
- Existing records, systems and permissions: provide references or mark unavailable.
- Constraints, capacity and reporting context: provide available facts or mark unknown.

### Process

1. Read README.md, AGENTS.md, the selected runbook(s), shared programme cycle, decision routing and human/AI responsibilities.
2. Capture confirmed facts, assumptions, missing information and proposed targets using the organisation profile. Ask only for missing facts needed for the next material decision; continue independent authorised work.
3. Resolve relevant source IDs through sources/catalogue.yaml. Read owning guidance and its qualifications before applying it. Use a consistent repository commit per work package and record retrieval time, section IDs, authority type and limitations. If retrieval is unavailable, record the affected conclusion and access limit rather than invent current guidance.
4. Establish scope and route applicable law, contract and adopted commitments for competent review. Do not assume employee/customer status alone determines a regime. Keep primary authority separate from advisory Double-FW methods.
5. Map complete activities, affected contexts, handoffs, supplier and content dependencies. Include disabled people's influence before and throughout evaluation. Synthetic personas can support exploration but cannot validate a real outcome.
6. Produce a feasible programme using P01–P09 and the relevant E/C steps. Cover regulation/policy, strategy/funding, procurement/architecture, design, engineering, content production, layered evaluation, release, operations, reporting and learning. Do not reproduce specialist source content.
7. Assign actual human R and single A per adopted activity; leave unknown roles unassigned. Propose A0–A3 per AI task with purpose, benefit, data/environment scope, permission, verification, stop and recovery. Keep legal, policy, budget, rights-affecting, material risk, external claim and release decisions with authorised humans.
8. Prioritise serious exclusion, critical activities, obligations, repeated/cumulative burden and dependencies. Offer interim access and permanent correction in parallel. Distinguish funded work from proposals and record consequences of deferral.
9. Create an evidence and reporting plan with baseline, methods, coverage, denominator, missingness, limits, source changes and review triggers. Preserve failures, unknowns and dissent. Do not claim compliance from a scan, satisfaction metric, generated plan or structural check.
10. End with concrete decisions required, evidence needed, named/unknown owners and the next authorised actions. Keep live confidential records in approved systems and use minimum necessary information.

### Explicit system and QA capability requirement

For both runbooks, plan accessible UX design systems and accessible branding systems using framework/design-and-brand-systems.md and templates/system-assurance-plan.md. Inventory existing/shared/vendor systems and plan adoption, creation, remediation or extension with owners, capacity, milestones and evidence. For every in-scope foundation/practice (including typography, colour, labelling, alternative text and content order), require best-practice references, manual tests and meaningful automatable checks or a justified manual-primary classification. For every pattern/component, require design requirements, technical techniques, methodologies, reference implementations and user-oriented ACT-style scripts, with IDs/revisions and consumer relationships. Assign missing assets as owned gaps rather than inventing them. Evaluate configured use and complete journeys, accessible usability and the six brand purposes separately from technical conformance. Preserve recognisability, distinctiveness, positioning, purpose and values; detailed guidance/assets remain in their owning systems.

### Required outputs

- Completed organisation profile with known facts and explicit gaps.
- Draft programme plan with selected runbooks, source-use register, outcomes, workstreams, resources, milestones/dependencies and proposed versus confirmed decisions.
- Human responsibility and bounded AI task/delegation plan.
- Priority barrier, evidence, interim access, correction and retest records.
- Team and leadership report views from the same evidence base.
- Source-change/review plan and clear adoption/execution status.

If no organisational facts are provided, create a clearly labelled generic configuration and progressive intake questions. Do not invent an approved organisation, budget, date, personnel or real evaluation result. Use the synthetic examples only as illustrations.
